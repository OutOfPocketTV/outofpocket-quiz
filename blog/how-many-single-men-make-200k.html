How many single men make $200k a year?

Out Of Pocket TV · November 18, 2026

About 1.6 million unmarried American men earn $200,000 or more a year. That’s about 1 in 38 unmarried men. And most men at that income are married: only about 1 in 5 isn’t.

We’ve covered single men earning $100k. This is the high end: $150,000, $200,000, $250,000 and up, counted from Census records that report income and marital status for the same people.

What stands out

  • Most $200,000 men are married. About 19% of men earning $200,000 or more aren’t. At 25 to 29 it’s about 63%, but by 35 to 39 it’s down to about 17%, and it stays between about 14% and 17% after that.
  • Among unmarried men, high incomes come later. Only about 0.3% of unmarried men under 25 earn $200,000. It’s around 3% in the thirties and peaks at about 5% between 55 and 64.
  • The pool shrinks fast as the bar rises. About 3.2 million unmarried men earn $150,000, 1.6 million earn $200,000, 780,000 earn $250,000 and about 200,000 earn $500,000. Up to $250,000, each step roughly halves it.
  • Add no kids and 6 feet, and it’s about 72,000 men. Unmarried, aged 25 to 40, earning $200,000 and 6 feet or taller with no children: about 1 man in 510 in that age range.

How these numbers are worked out

Age, income and marital status are counted together, from the same people, in the Census Bureau’s Current Population Survey (March 2025, about 108,000 adults), weighted to represent the U.S. Income is total personal income before tax for 2024, not household income. The higher the income, the fewer survey records each figure rests on: the $250,000 and $500,000 rows are the least precise, and $500,000 is too thin to split by age. “Not married” means never married, divorced, widowed or separated, and isn’t the same as single. Children and height in the last table come from other surveys and are applied on top for each age; taller men earn slightly more on average, so the last row is probably a little low.
