What percentage of women are 5′9″ or taller?

Out Of Pocket TV · November 23, 2026

About 1 in 41 American women is 5′9″ or taller: 2.4% of women aged 18 to 80, or roughly 3.1 million women. About 1.7 million of them aren’t married.

A 5′9″ woman is about as rare as a 6′3″ man. Here is how many women there are at each height from 5′6″ up, how many of them are single, and how it changes with age.

What stands out

  • Each inch above 5′7″ roughly halves the pool, or worse. About 1 woman in 9 is 5′7″ or taller, 1 in 19 is 5′8″, 1 in 41 is 5′9″, about 1 in 100 is 5′10″ and about 1 in 880 is 6 feet.
  • A 5′9″ woman is about as rare as a 6′3″ man. 2.4% of women are 5′9″ or taller and 2.3% of men are 6′3″ or taller.
  • Younger women are taller. About 1 woman in 26 under 30 is 5′9″ or taller, against about 1 in 120 aged 65 to 80.
  • Most tall women under 30 are single. About 810,000 of the roughly 990,000 women under 30 who are 5′9″ or taller aren’t married.

How these numbers are worked out

Height comes from the CDC’s National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES, 2021–2023), which measures people without shoes rather than asking them. The calculator fits a bell curve to each age group’s measured heights, and this far above the average the curve and the direct count part company a little: the measured count on our height odds page puts women 5′9″ or taller at about 1 in 50, a bit rarer than the curve’s 1 in 41. Treat the tallest rows as estimates. Marital status comes from the Census Bureau’s Current Population Survey (March 2025); “not married” includes divorced, widowed and separated women and isn’t the same as single.
