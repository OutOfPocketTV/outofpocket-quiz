How many men are 6 feet tall and make six figures?

Out Of Pocket TV · October 27, 2026

About 1 in 27 American men is 6 feet or taller and earns $100,000 or more a year: 3.7% of men aged 18 to 80, or roughly 4.6 million people. Require that he isn’t married and it falls to 0.93%, about 1 man in 108.

“6 foot, 6 figures” is the most famous dating standard on the internet, and it comes up in almost every street interview we film. So we ran it through the calculator, using the Census Bureau’s 2025 income records and the CDC’s measured heights.

The two halves on their own

  • 6 feet or taller: 15.6% of men aged 18 to 80, about 1 in 6. The CDC measured them without shoes, so this isn’t the dating-app version of 6 feet.
  • $100,000 or more: 23.6% of men, about 1 in 4. That’s total personal income before tax, not household income.

Put them together and only about 1 man in 27 has both.

What stands out

  • Under 25, it’s almost nobody. Only about 2% of men aged 18 to 24 earn $100,000, so 6 foot, 6 figures at that age is fewer than 1 in 250.
  • The pool of men with both peaks around 40. Men in their early forties are the most likely to clear both bars (6.7%), because incomes keep rising into the forties.
  • But the single ones peak in their late twenties. Once you add “not married”, 25 to 29 is the best age group (1.7%), and it drops with every decade after. Most men who are tall and earning six figures by 40 are already married.
  • Multiplying the percentages gets it wrong. For men aged 25 to 35, 6 feet is 18.2%, $100k is 20.1% and not married is 63.9%. Multiply them and you’d expect 2.3%. The real figure is 1.60%, because married men earn more: counted from the same people, only 8.8% of men that age are both unmarried and earning $100,000.

How these numbers are worked out

Age, income and marital status are counted together from the Census Bureau’s Current Population Survey (March 2025, about 108,000 adults). Height comes from the CDC’s National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (2021–2023) for each age group and is applied on top. Taller men do earn slightly more on average, so the true figure for height plus income is probably a little higher than shown. “Not married” includes never married, divorced, widowed and separated, and isn’t the same as single.
