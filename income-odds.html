How many people earn $100k a year?

Income odds for men and women · Last updated: September 22, 2026

About 1 in 4 American men and 1 in 8 women have a personal income of $100,000 or more a year. Raise that bar to $200,000 and the pool of men shrinks by nearly three-quarters.

What stands out

  • The typical man has an income of about $50,000 and the typical woman about $32,000. Those are medians across all adults (half have more, half have less), so any minimum above them rules out most people.
  • $100,000 is roughly the top quarter of men, but only 1 man in 50 under 25.
  • Each step up costs more than it looks. Going from $100,000 to $150,000 roughly halves the share of men who qualify, and at $250,000 it’s about 1 in 25.
  • Seven-figure incomes are about 1 man in 280.

How these numbers are worked out

These are counts, not a curve: the share of the roughly 108,000 adults in the U.S. Census Bureau’s Current Population Survey (March 2025) whose total personal income for 2024 was at or above each level, weighted to represent the U.S. The calculator uses the same records, counted together with age, race and marital status, so “unmarried and earning $100,000 at 30” is also a real count.

This is total personal income before tax (wages plus business, investment and benefit income), not household income. It includes adults with no income at all (6.6% of men, 10.5% of women). The $500,000+ and $1,000,000+ rows rest on a few hundred people each, so they’re the least precise.
